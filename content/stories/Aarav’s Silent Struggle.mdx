title: "Aarav’s Silent Struggle"
date: "2025-05-02"
excerpt: "Grew up Hindu in NJ, found college solace but stays closeted at home."
---

I grew up in a devout Hindu household in suburban New Jersey, where temple bells and family prayers shaped every weekend.  
From childhood, I felt a persistent hesitation in my chest whenever family members spoke of marriage and grandchildren.  
In high school, I excelled academically but struggled to concentrate, my mind wandering to crushes I dared not name.  
I buried those feelings beneath academic achievements and community service, convinced duty would silence my truth.

Entering college was like stepping into a new world.  
I joined a South Asian student group, attended cultural festivals, and for the first time felt both familiar and free.  
One evening at a welcome event, I saw Ravi laugh with friends—an easy, effortless joy I envied and longed for.  
My heart fluttered, and I realized I was in love.

I found refuge in the LGBTQ+ affinity group on campus, where discussions ranged from coming-out stories to queer representation in cinema.  
In that room, the word “gay” felt less like danger and more like home.  
I shared my first secret with my roommate, who hugged me and said, “We’ve got you.”  
I cried with relief, finally feeling seen.

Back home, I keep my secret well-guarded.  
At family dinners, I listen as relatives ask, “When will you bring home someone?”  
My smile never reaches my eyes.  
I respond with vague answers and quickly change the topic.

Each festival brings a new test of my resolve.  
I wear the traditional kurta, greet elders with respect, and slip into prayer, hoping faith will protect me.  
At night, I text Ravi about my fear of rejection, and he reminds me that love thrives in honesty, even if slowly revealed.  
One day, I hope to stand before my family and say, “This is who I am,” without fear.
