---
title: "Fatima’s Quiet Identity"
date: "2025-05-02"
excerpt: "Queer non-binary teen in a Muslim household, finding voice online."
---

I was born into a family where prayer is woven into our daily lives and modesty shapes every interaction.  
My reflection in the mirror often felt incomplete—neither fully boy nor girl.  
I learned early to conceal my uncertainty beneath layers of fabric and polite smiles.

Behind a locked screen, I created the pseudonym “Zahra” to share poetry and art in online communities.  
Under that name, I wrote lines like, “I am a tapestry of stars and quiet storms,” and posted them to small groups of queer Muslims who understood my contradictions.  
Their comments felt like whispered affirmations in a silent room.

Every morning, I perform my duties with care—attending classes, helping my younger siblings, reciting Quranic verses.  
But each evening, I return to my safe space—a tiny corner of my room illuminated by a single lamp.  
There, I continue the dual life of Fatima by day and Zahra by night.

The fear of being discovered hangs over me like a heavy curtain.  
I imagine my parents’ shock, my community’s disapproval, and the potential exile from everything I know.  
And yet, the poetry I write brings me closer to understanding my own heart.

I find strength in queer Muslim creators who blend faith and identity in their videos.  
When one vlogger says, “Your identity is not an insult to your faith,” I replay it a dozen times.  
Those words become a silent prayer I carry with me through the day.

Someday, I hope to speak these truths aloud—to gather courage beyond the keyboard and share my real name.  
Until then, I remain hidden in plain sight, my spirit flickering between devotion and self-discovery.
