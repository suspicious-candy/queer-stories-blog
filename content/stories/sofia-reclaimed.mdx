---
title: "Sofia’s Reclaimed Self"
date: "2025-05-02"
excerpt: "Left a brief arranged marriage, now reclaiming identity in a new city."
---

My arranged marriage at twenty-two felt like a gilded cage—lovely from the outside, suffocating within.  
In my husband’s eyes, I saw confusion rather than love, and realized my heart belonged to women.

I left without fanfare, carrying only a suitcase and my secret.  
Seattle’s skyline greeted me with open arms, and I rented a small studio in Capitol Hill, determined to rebuild.

I started an anonymous blog under the name “River,” pouring out memories of silk saris, stolen glances, and the guilt that shadowed every prayer.  
Followers wrote back, sharing similar journeys and thanking me for my honesty.

Each morning, I brew strong coffee and write for hours, reconstructing my identity from fragments of hope and fear.  
Afternoons, I volunteer at a women’s shelter, listening to stories of survival and transformation.

My greatest milestone came when I read my first live poem at an open mic.  
Lines about silk, rebellion, and reclaimed names echoed through the room, and applause felt like an act of liberation.

I call my parents every Sunday, gently telling them I am well.  
I keep my new life partially obscured, but I no longer apologize for breathing.

One day, I hope to stand before them as Sofia—whole, free, and in love with who I’ve become.
