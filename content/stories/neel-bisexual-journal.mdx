date: "2025-05-02"

My Catholic high school was a place of strict uniforms and ritual prayers, where differences were hushed into silence.  
I discovered my bisexuality quietly, through stolen glances and whispered crushes on both male and female friends.  
I had no label until I stumbled across a blog post one night titled “When you love more than one gender,” and felt tears of recognition.

I began writing in a spiral notebook—entries about my confusion, longing, and the fear of being ‘too much’ or ‘not enough.’  
I illustrated my feelings in zines that I photocopied in the library and slipped into my friends’ lockers.  
They responded with hand-drawn hearts and notes that said, “You are valid.”

At school, jokes about being “confused” stung like needles.  
I plastered a small “Bi & Proud” sticker on my tablet’s cover, but hid it under textbooks when teachers walked by.  
Every morning felt like a negotiation between authenticity and safety.

During summer break, I attended a local Pride march in disguise—jeans, hoodie, and a borrowed rainbow wristband.  
For one day, I felt fully seen, marching alongside strangers who cheered my existence without question.

Back home, I return to the weight of expectation.  
My parents speak of arranged marriages and grandchildren, unaware of the heart that beats differently.  
I write new journal entries about how love can be expansive—embracing all its forms.

One day, I’ll share my truth in that silent chapel of my high school, but for now, I carry my identity in pages turned by hopeful hands.
